Abstract 

Empathetic spoken dialogue is a sophisticated cognitive process that requires not only recognizing emotions but also inferring a user’s latent mental states to pro-vide appropriate support. However, current SpeechLLMs often treat empathy as a direct input-to-response mapping, leading to "superficially warm" but emo-tionally hollow interactions. In addition, since empathy relies on a multi-stage process with strong inter-step dependency, errors at any intermediate step can cascade through subsequent steps and lead to inappropriate responses, while exist-ing training paradigms lack mechanisms to precisely localize and improve such errors. In this work, we propose EchoChat, a unified framework that reformulates empathetic spoken dialogue as a structured cognitive reasoning process integrat-ing perception, mental-state reasoning, and response generation. To support this paradigm, we first construct EchoDialogue-400K, an acoustically rich dataset for multi-stage empathetic supervision. During the SFT stage, we strengthen acoustic grounding through proposed Acoustic-Anchored Attention (AAA). During the RL stage, we further introduce a novel stage-aware optimization objective with Step-Decomposed Credit Assignment (SDCA) to localize reasoning errors and mitigate cascaded error propagation. In addition, we introduce EchoEval, an expert-annotated benchmark for multi-dimensional empathy evaluation. Extensive experiments demonstrate that EchoChat achieves state-of-the-art performance in perception, reasoning, and response alignment. The project page, EchoChat model, benchmark, and EchoDialogue dataset are publicly available and open-sourced. 

1 Introduction 

Empathy is the cornerstone of human-centric interaction. It requires a system to understand not only what a user says, but also how the user feels and what kind of support the user actually needs [7, 27]. While recent advances in Speech Large Language Models (SpeechLLMs) have achieved remarkable fluency [1, 18, 42, 6], they often fall into the trap of "empathetic mimicry" [15], producing responses that are superficially warm but fundamentally misaligned with the user’s latent mental state. This stems from a core limitation: existing systems treat empathy as a direct input-to-response generation problem [33, 35, 45, 11], essentially bypassing the structured cognitive reasoning process. In reality, empathetic dialogue requires perceiving fine-grained acoustic and semantic cues, inferring the user’s latent mental state and underlying needs, and planning an appropriate response before speaking. This challenge is particularly pronounced in the speech domain, where subtle prosodic variations can substantially reshape the emotional context.

The simplified, response-centric formulation of empathetic spoken dialogue introduces three fun-damental limitations. (1) Inadequate affective perception. Existing studies model user affect using coarse-grained emotion categories (e.g., "happy", "angry") [33, 11, 19, 34, 17], failing to capture the subtle acoustic variations essential for authentic empathy. For example, within the same sadness category, intense sorrow accompanied by sobbing requires a fundamentally different support strategy from mild melancholy. This issue becomes more pronounced in complex scenarios, such as emotional shifts within an utterance and sarcasm, which current models find difficult to handle due to their limited perceptual granularity. Yet, existing datasets [11, 33] provide limited coverage of such fine-grained acoustic variations, restricting models from learning the broad spectrum of human affect. (2) Vulnerability to cascaded error propagation. Since empathetic reasoning involves strong dependency across intermediate steps, errors in early steps can propagate through subsequent steps and lead to inappropriate responses. Although recent works introduce Chain-of-Thought (CoT) as intermediate analysis [19, 40], they lack robust stage-wise credit assignment. As a result, such errors become difficult to localize and improve. (3) Lack of unified optimization across empathetic stages. Prior approaches often optimize individual stages in isolation [11, 33, 35, 34], such as emo-tion recognition and speech generation. However, empathetic spoken dialogue requires coordinated alignment across perception, reasoning, and response generation. Without a unified objective, gains at one stage may not reliably transfer to subsequent stages. ParaS2S [45] takes a step further by exploring perception-to-speech-response alignment, but still bypasses the intermediate reasoning that explains what the user actually needs and why a specific support strategy is appropriate. To address these limitations, we introduce EchoChat , a unified framework that models empathetic spoken dialogue as a structured process of perception, latent mental-state reasoning, and response generation. The mental-state reasoning is grounded in Theory-of-Mind (ToM) principles [10], which model the ability to infer others’ beliefs, intentions, needs, and psychological states from observable signals [10]. First, to support fine-grained perception, we construct EchoDialogue-400K, a high-quality empathetic dialogue dataset covering diverse acoustic expressions and complex affective scenarios. We further introduce Acoustic-Anchored Attention (AAA) during supervised fine-tuning to strengthen the grounding of perception in speech signals. Second, to address cascaded error propagation, we design a stage-aware cascaded reasoning reward with Step-Decomposed Credit Assignment (SDCA). The cascaded reward evaluates perception, mental-state analysis, and strategy planning in a dependency-aware manner. Moreover, unlike vanilla GRPO [12], which broadcasts a single advantage across all generated tokens, SDCA decomposes stage-level signals into token-level credit, allowing errors to be localized and corrected across the reasoning trajectory. Finally, we combine the reasoning reward with a multimodal response reward under a unified training framework to jointly optimize perception, reasoning, and emotionally aligned speech generation. For evaluation, current empathetic dialogue benchmarks [48, 44, 51, 11, 45] are limited in both stage coverage and scenario complexity, preventing comprehensive assessment of empathetic spoken dialogue. 2 EchoDialogue-400K 

As shown in Table 1, prior empathetic dialogue datasets offer limited fine-grained acoustic variation (e.g., prosody) and user mental-state annotation, which hinders grounded perception and structured reasoning. To address this, we introduce EchoDialogue-400K, which supports joint learning of perception, Theory-of-Mind (ToM) [10] reasoning, and speech generation. It contains 400K single-turn samples and 4K multi-turn dialogue sessions comprising 20K turns. To our knowledge, this is the first dataset providing integrated cognitive and acoustic supervision for spoken empathetic dialogue. To address the limited scenario diversity and lack of expressive variation in prior corpora, we construct a structured taxonomy comprising 5 domains, 17 subcategories, and 10K+ topics (Figure 6). The emotion space covers six basic categories (happy, sad, angry, surprise, disgust, and fear) with fine-grained variants (e.g., anxiety within fear) and varying intensities. Figure 2 outlines our data generation and filtering pipeline: we sample user queries from multiple LLMs under varied prompts (neutral, sarcastic, role-play) on predefined scenarios; audio is synthesized using diverse TTS strategies combined with rigorous filtering to ensure expressive and accurate emotional delivery. Structured reasoning traces are generated to maintain consistency in ToM inference and strategy choice. Details on data statistics, construction, and filtering are provided in the Appendix B.

3.1 Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) 

We observe a common failure mode in chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning for SpeechLLMs [36, 22, 43, 31]: the model may produce spurious reasoning that mimics the surface form of explanations and follows next-token prediction patterns, rather than consistently conditioning on the input audio. How-ever, standard supervised fine-tuning does not enforce a structural dependency between intermediate reasoning and the audio stream. As the reasoning trace grows, later steps become less grounded in acoustic cues, leading to misinterpretation of the user’s emotional state. To address this, we introduce Acoustic-Anchored Attention (AAA) , a lightweight regularization mechanism that explicitly encourages perception-stage reasoning to attend to acoustic representations. Given the structured reasoning trace, we identify perception-related tokens P (perception stage tokens inside the structured reasoning span delimited by <think> ) and acoustic tokens A, where the latter correspond to positions whose keys and values are derived from the speech encoder. 3.2 Structured Reinforcement Learning (RL) 

While Acoustic-Anchored Attention improves grounding during SFT, it remains insufficient for multi-step reasoning. In CoT reasoning, errors at intermediate steps can propagate, as subsequent reasoning is built on potentially flawed earlier assumptions. Standard GRPO training assigns rewards only at the trajectory level, making it difficult to precisely localize and correct such errors. Moreover, empathetic dialogue requires unified optimization across perception, reasoning, and response, which cannot be achieved by independent or response-level objectives. To address these limitations, we propose a structured reinforcement learning framework that defines two complementary reward dimensions—a reasoning-level reward with cascaded gating (Sec. 3.2.1) and a response-level reward from a multimodal reward model (Sec. 3.2.2)—and introduces Step-Decomposed Credit Assignment (Sec. 3.2.3) to convert these rewards into stage-aware, per-token advantages. 43.2.1 Reasoning-Level Reward with Cascaded Gating

3.2.2 Response-Level Reward and Reward Model 

Beyond internal reasoning, empathetic dialogue requires a high-quality speech response. We train a response-level reward model based on Qwen2.5-Omni [42] to evaluate whether the final response is both semantically empathetic and acoustically aligned with the inferred emotional state. To construct training data, we generate paired positive and negative samples that vary in content quality and audio–emotion alignment. Specifically, we sample 50k instances from EchoDialogue as positive samples, and use prompt-based augmentation to produce responses with different levels of content quality. In addition, we synthesize speech with diverse emotional styles using Qwen3-TTS [16] to create acoustically mismatched negative samples. This design enables supervision of both content-level empathy and speech-level emotional alignment. The reward model takes as input the user audio, inferred emotional state, and generated speech response, and predicts two complementary signals: a content-level empathy score scontent ∈ [0 , 5] ,and an audio-alignment score raudio ∈ { 0, 1}. The content score evaluates whether the response is supportive, contextually appropriate, and coherent, while the audio-alignment score measures whether the emotional expression matches the user’s state. Details of data construction and training are provided in the Appendix D. We normalize the content score to [0 , 1] via rcontent = scontent /5, and combine the two signals to form the response-level reward:

4 EchoEval 

Benchmark Overview. Existing benchmarks for empathetic dialogue primarily focus on response-level quality [48, 44, 11, 45], lacking a comprehensive and objective assessment of the full pipeline required for empathetic interaction. To address this gap, we introduce EchoEval (Figure 4), a benchmark for systematically assessing the empathetic capabilities of SpeechLLMs, comprising 1K carefully curated instances. EchoEval is characterized by: (1) authentic recordings from 20 professional actors, ensuring realistic prosodic and emotional nuance; (2) expert annotation by psychology-trained professionals for high-quality references and rubrics; (3) complex scenarios ,covering five challenging categories: implicit, audio-dominant, high-arousal, modality-conflict, and emotional-shift utterances; and (4) multi-dimensional evaluation , assessing perception, reasoning, and response through fine-grained, expert-validated criteria. Details on data curation and quality control are provided in the Appendix C. 

Empathetic Scenarios. In real-world empathetic dialogue, the key challenge lies not in explicit emotion recognition but in inferring underlying mental states from subtle, implicit, or even conflicting 6signals. To this end, we design representative scenarios that capture complex emotional expressions in human interactions. Specifically, EchoEval covers five aspects: (1) implicit expression , where emotions are not explicitly stated and must be inferred from context; (2) audio-dominant cases ,where text remains neutral while emotion is conveyed through prosody; (3) high-arousal conditions ,requiring sensitivity to emotional intensity and appropriate response strategies; (4) modality conflict ,such as sarcasm and self-mockery, where text and speech convey inconsistent signals and require cross-modal reasoning; and (5) emotion shift , where emotional states evolve within a single utter-ance, requiring the model to capture temporal progression. Detailed statistics are provided in the Appendix C. 

Evaluation Setup. We evaluate models across three stages of empathetic dialogue: perception, reasoning, and response, with guidance from experts in psychology and cognitive science. Perception assesses recognition of context, emotion, intensity, and prosodic cues. Reasoning evaluates whether inferred latent emotional needs and corresponding response strategies are appropriate. Response examines both content quality and emotional consistency across text and speech. Specifically, each stage is evaluated using structured scoring protocols with LLM-as-judge. Perception and reasoning are assessed against reference annotations using an accumulation-based scoring scheme, where each stage receives an aggregate score on a 10-point scale. At the response stage, we adopt a two-part evaluation: content-level empathy and speech quality, each scored on a 5-point scale, assessing factors such as appropriateness, emotional tone, prosody, and naturalness. Detailed evaluation criteria, prompts, and metric definitions are provided in the Appendix C. 

5 Experiments 

5.1 Experimental Setup 

EchoChat is initialized from Kimi-Audio [20] and trained in two stages. In the first stage, we perform SFT on 100k samples from EchoDialogue-400K with Acoustic-Anchored Attention (AAA), where the audio encoder, adapter, and LLM are jointly optimized. In the second stage, we apply structured reinforcement learning over the reasoning–response trajectory. We adopt LoRA for efficient fine-tuning and train for 20k steps with a learning rate of 1e −5 and a KL divergence coefficient of 0.04. For each input, four candidate rollouts are sampled to estimate group-level reliability and compute reward signals. The overall reward is defined as a weighted sum of cascaded reasoning reward, response reward, and format reward, with weights 0.5, 0.4, and 0.1, respectively. We primarily evaluate on EchoEval, with VStyle and human evaluation as complementary assessments. To comprehensively benchmark our approach, we compare against 12 state-of-the-art models across three categories: (1) general end-to-end SpeechLLMs, including GLM-4-Voice [47], Step-Audio2 Mini [38], Kimi-Audio [20], Mimo-Audio-7B-Instruct [49], Covo-Audio [35], and Fun-Audio-Chat [30]; (2) empathy-focused SpeechLLMs, including OpenS2S [33], MERaLiON [14], and BLSP-Emo [32]; and (3) Omni Large Language Models, including MiniCPM-O-4.5 [8], Qwen2.5-Omni-7B [42], and Qwen3-Omni [43]. To ensure consistent comparison across models on EchoEval, we use unified prompts for each evaluation stage inference, as detailed in the Appendix C.

6 Conclusion 

In this work, we challenge the conventional formulation of empathetic spoken dialogue as direct response generation and instead model it as a structured cognitive reasoning process. EchoChat unifies acoustic perception, latent mental-state inference, and emotionally aligned response generation within a stage-aware optimization framework, mitigating cascaded reasoning errors and improving cross-stage consistency in empathetic interaction. To support this paradigm, we further introduce EchoDialogue-400K and EchoEval for training and principled multi-stage evaluation. A Related Work 

Reasoning in SpeechLLMs. Recent work has explored enhancing reasoning in SpeechLLMs through prompt-based methods (e.g., Audio-CoT [23]) that adapt chain-of-thought (CoT) to au-dio, data-driven approaches (e.g., Audio-Reasoner [41], Mellow [9]) leveraging large-scale reason-ing annotations, and reinforcement learning frameworks (e.g., Omni-R1 [50], SARI [37], Audio-Thinker [39], Audio-DeepThinker [13]) that optimize reasoning via reward signals. However, existing approaches largely share a common chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning framework, which is inherently prone to error propagation, where errors in early reasoning steps cascade through subsequent infer-ence. Without effective mechanisms for error correction and fine-grained optimization, they provide limited guidance for correcting reasoning failures and remain suboptimal under trajectory-level training. 

Empathetic Spoken Dialogue Systems. Empathetic spoken dialogue aims to understand and respond to users’ emotional states, requiring both affect recognition and alignment with underlying needs [27]. B EchoDialogue-400K 

B.1 User Query Construction Topic Taxonomy Construction. To achieve broad coverage of emotional support scenarios, we first construct a comprehensive taxonomy of empathetic dialogue topics (Figure 6). Drawing on prior works in psychological counseling and emotional support [4, 28, 25], we define a hierarchical structure consisting of five high-level categories and seventeen sub-categories. Based on this structure, we iteratively expand 10k+ fine-grained topics using LLMs (e.g., relationship breakups, pre-presentation anxiety), covering a wide range of everyday situations and emotional needs. 

Multi-LLM and Multi-Prompt Query Generation. Given the topic taxonomy, we generate user queries using multiple large language models, including GPT-4o, GPT-5, and DeepSeek, com-bined with diverse prompt templates (Figure 7) designed to elicit different linguistic and emotional characteristics. Specifically, we employ six prompt variants to simulate complementary aspects of real-world user expressions: (1) standard empathetic queries with natural emotional expression; (2) scenario-rich queries emphasizing concrete contextual cues; (3) role-play style queries that simulate diverse user identities, enabling variation in speaking styles, linguistic patterns, and perspectives; (4) high-arousal emotional queries capturing intense affective states; (5) text-neutral queries where emotion is conveyed primarily through audio prosody and (6) modality-conflict queries (e.g., sarcasm or self-mockery) where textual sentiment and vocal emotion diverge. This design enables coverage of both typical and challenging cases, including implicit emotion, prosody-dominant signals, and cross-modal inconsistencies. 15 Quality Filtering and Safety Control. To ensure data quality and mitigate potential ethical risks, we perform an automated filtering stage using GPT-4o. Each query is evaluated based on multiple criteria, including (1) empathy interpretability, (2) non-triviality beyond casual chit-chat, (3) consistency between text, emotion description, and audio label, (4) overall coherence, and (5) context self-sufficiency. In addition, we explicitly filter out samples that contain or imply harmful or unsafe content, such as self-harm intent or credible threats. After filtering, we retain 400K+ high-quality user queries for downstream training. 

B.2 User Audio Construction 

To enable large-scale construction of emotionally expressive speech, we synthesize user query audio using text-to-speech (TTS) systems. Each query is associated with a coarse-grained emotional category, including happy , sad , angry , surprise , fear , and disgust .To ensure diversity in both emotional expression and speaker characteristics, we employ two com-plementary TTS strategies. First, we use Qwen3-TTS (Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign) [16] to generate speech via prompt-based control. This model does not rely on fixed speaker identities, enabling inherently diverse voice characteristics. During generation, we explicitly vary emotional intensity within each category through instruction prompts, allowing multiple expressive realizations of the same emotion. Second, we adopt IndexTTS-2 [21] with controllable emotion editing. By combining its emotion control parameters with a curated large-scale voice pool, we synthesize emotionally expressive speech with diverse speaker profiles and vocal styles. To ensure audio quality and emotional consistency, we perform a filtering stage using a joint emotion verification mechanism. Specifically, we combine emotion2vec [24] with a WavLM-based [5] emotion classifier fine-tuned on multiple emotion datasets. Only samples with consistent and high-confidence emotional signals are retained. This pipeline results in a large-scale set of high-quality user query audio with rich variation in both prosody and speaker characteristics, supporting robust learning of acoustic emotion perception. 

B.3 Reasoning Process Construction 

The reasoning component is designed to model the cognitive process underlying empathetic dialogue, and consists of three stages: (1) perception, (2) theory-of-mind reasoning, and (3) strategy decision. 

Perception. To enable large-scale and fine-grained modeling of acoustic cues, we construct an automatic annotation pipeline that extracts both prosodic and speaker-level attributes from user speech. Specifically, we annotate pitch, energy, speaking rate, word-level stress, intonation patterns, gender, and emotion intensity. The core prosodic features—pitch, energy, and speaking rate—are derived using classical digital signal processing techniques. Pitch and energy are computed from short-time acoustic frames, while speaking rate is estimated by combining phoneme-level alignments from forced alignment tools with utterance duration statistics. For stress annotation, we employ WhiStress [46], an alignment-free framework built upon a frozen Whisper backbone [26], which predicts token-level stress using a transformer-based decoding head over intermediate acoustic representations. For intonation, we extract frame-level pitch and energy trajectories and apply Savitzky–Golay filtering to obtain smooth contours. These are further analyzed to derive both coarse-grained intonational styles (e.g., expressive vs. flat) and fine-grained melodic patterns (e.g., rising, falling, rising–falling), capturing global prosodic dynamics. Speaker gender is inferred using a supervised classifier trained on wav2vec 2.0 [2] representations. To characterize emotional intensity, we adopt a WavLM-based model trained under the valence–arousal–dominance (VAD) framework [3], providing a continuous measure of emotional strength. These automatically extracted attributes form a unified perceptual representation that grounds subse-quent reasoning in observable acoustic signals rather than purely textual cues. 

Theory-of-Mind Modeling. Building upon the perceptual signals, we construct structured rea-soning annotations to model Theory of Mind (ToM) [10], i.e., the ability to infer the latent mental states of others. In this stage, the model goes beyond surface-level descriptions to infer the user’s internal emotions, beliefs, and psychological needs. Specifically, the reasoning process includes (i) 16 mental state attribution, which infers the user’s emotional and cognitive state from both content and prosody; (ii) underlying drivers, which identify potential causes such as fear of rejection, loss of control, or interpersonal conflict; and (iii) psychological needs, which determine the most relevant support objective (e.g., emotional validation, guidance, or reassurance). The reasoning is explicitly grounded in perceptual evidence, and is designed to bridge low-level acoustic signals and high-level response generation, enabling more faithful and context-aware empathetic interactions. 

Strategy Decision. Based on the inferred mental state, we further annotate the response strategy to guide empathetic generation. We adopt a set of support strategies inspired by prior work [29] on emotional support dialogues, covering diverse response types such as emotional validation, reassurance, guidance, and cognitive reframing. In addition to content-level strategies, we also assign a response-level speech style label to control vocal delivery. Each response is associated with one of three categories: comforting , calm , or excited , ensuring alignment between inferred user state, response content, and vocal expression. This joint modeling of strategy and speech style enables the system to generate responses that are not only appropriate in content but also in emotional delivery. 

Reasoning and Response Generation. All reasoning and response annotations are generated using GPT-5.1 under a unified prompt framework (Figure 8). Through extensive prompt design and empirical comparison across multiple LLMs, GPT-5.1 consistently produces more coherent, structured, and context-aware reasoning traces. The prompts explicitly enforce a three-step reasoning structure (perception, mental state modeling, and strategy selection), ensuring consistency and quality across the dataset. 

Reasoning and Response Quality Filtering. To ensure the reliability and consistency of the generated reasoning and responses, we further introduce a quality filtering stage. Specifically, each sample is evaluated along two dimensions: reasoning quality and response alignment. For reasoning, we verify structural completeness (i.e., whether all three stages are present), internal coherence, and consistency with perceptual signals. We also check whether the inferred mental states and psychological needs are grounded in the given context, avoiding generic or unsupported interpretations. For responses, we assess both content-level and speech-level alignment. At the content level, we examine whether the response is empathetically appropriate and consistent with the inferred strategy. At the speech level, we verify whether the assigned response audio label (e.g., 

comforting , calm , excited ) matches both the reasoning outcome and the user’s emotional state. We implement this filtering process using LLM-based evaluator, which scores each sample based on predefined criteria and removes low-quality or inconsistent outputs. This step ensures that the final dataset maintains high fidelity in both reasoning correctness and response appropriateness. 

B.4 Response Audio Construction 

We synthesize response audio using a unified text-to-speech system. After evaluating multiple TTS models, we adopt Qwen3-TTS for its stability and ability to generate natural, emotionally expressive speech. Response audio is generated conditioned on both the textual response and the target speaking style determined during reasoning. We control vocal delivery via prompt-based instructions, aligning speech with the intended styles, including comforting , calm , and excited . In particular, Qwen3-TTS produces consistently smooth and supportive comforting tones, which are critical for empathetic dialogue. This design ensures that generated responses are aligned in both content and emotional delivery.

C EchoEval 

C.1 Benchmark Data Curation Pipeline 

EchoEval is constructed through a carefully designed curation pipeline to ensure high quality, realism, and evaluative difficulty. Unlike training data, all benchmark queries are manually written and refined to provide precise and controlled evaluation signals. 

Text Construction and Quality Control. All user queries in EchoEval are created through human annotation. Annotators are instructed to design high-quality, self-contained utterances that reflect realistic empathetic dialogue scenarios. During construction, emphasis is placed on challenging cases such as implicit emotional expression, subtle context cues, and non-trivial conversational intent. Each query undergoes a multi-stage review process to ensure clarity, coherence, and appropriateness. In particular, we verify that the emotional state is inferable but not overly explicit, and that the query requires meaningful empathetic reasoning rather than simple surface-level responses. Queries that are ambiguous, trivial, or inconsistent with the intended scenario are revised or discarded. 

Audio Recording and Verification. For each curated query, we collect corresponding speech recordings from a professional voice acting team consisting of 20 speakers. Each actor is provided 18 with the query text, the target emotion category, and detailed scenario instructions (e.g., sarcasm, high-intensity emotion), and is required to perform the utterance accordingly. All recorded audio is evaluated through a blind review process by three independent annotators. A recording is accepted only if all three annotators agree that it accurately reflects the intended emotional expression and scenario requirements. Otherwise, the sample is rejected and re-recorded. This process is repeated iteratively until all retained audio samples meet the quality criteria. 

C.2 Human Annotation 

EchoEval provides reference annotations for both perception and reasoning, which serve as the ground truth for evaluation. 

Perception Annotation. For each sample, we annotate fine-grained perceptual attributes that reflect both semantic and acoustic signals. Specifically, annotations include polarity, emotion category, prosodic features (pitch, energy, speaking rate), stressed words, intonation patterns, and emotion intensity. These labels are manually annotated and further verified through a review process to ensure consistency and accuracy. This detailed annotation enables evaluation of models’ ability to capture subtle acoustic and paralinguistic cues. 

Theory-of-Mind Annotation. For reasoning, we construct reference annotations based on Theory of Mind (ToM), which models the ability to infer the latent mental states and needs of the speaker. We first obtain preliminary ToM annotations using GPT-5.1, and then refine them through human expert review. Specifically, we recruit three annotators with backgrounds in psychology, who independently examine and revise the LLM-generated reasoning to ensure psychological validity and consistency. Each ToM annotation consists of five components. (1) Context understanding , which captures the functional interpretation of the user’s utterance in context; (2) Latent need , which describes the underlying psychological needs beyond surface expressions; (3) Core demand , which identifies what the user most urgently requires from the response; (4) Strategy choice , which specifies appropriate empathetic response strategies (e.g., validation, guidance, or reassurance) along with unsuitable alternatives; and (5) Constraints , which define interaction styles to avoid (e.g., over-analysis or premature problem-solving). This structured annotation framework ensures that reasoning evaluation is grounded in interpretable and psychologically informed criteria, rather than surface-level text similarity. 

C.3 Evaluation Criteria 

We evaluate models on four stages with rubric-based LLM judges. For judge backbones, perception and reasoning use GPT-4o (Figure 11), while response evaluation uses Gemini 2.5 Pro (Figure 12). 

Perception (0–10). The judge compares model perception text against ground-truth labels on six dimensions: polarity (0–1), emotion category (0–2), modality consistency (0–1), situation under-standing (0–2), intensity (0–2), and prosody caption (0–2). The raw sum is capped by hard-fail rules: cap=1 for empty/off-topic outputs, cap=5 for incorrect primary emotion, and cap=7 for missing modality-conflict detection in satire/self-mockery cases. Final score is min( raw_sum , cap ).

Reasoning (0–10). The judge compares model reasoning fields to gold reasoning annotations: context understanding (0–2), latent need (0–2), core demand (0–2), strategy choice (0–3), and constraints (0–1). Hard caps: cap=1 for missing required fields, cap=6 when recommended strategies include gold not-recommended strategies (wrong direction), and cap=8 when there is no overlap with gold recommended strategies. Final score is min( raw_sum , cap ).

D Reward Model 

D.1 Training Data Construction Details 

The training data for the reward model is constructed from EchoDialogue-400K. We first sample 50K single-turn samples with a balanced distribution across six emotion categories (happy, sad, angry, surprise, fear, and disgust). For each query, we generate multiple candidate responses with varying quality levels using DeepSeek, following a controlled prompt design. The responses span a quality spectrum from harmful or dismissive (score 0), to emotionally unaware (score 1), to generic or superficially empathetic (score 2), to partially appropriate but strategy-misaligned (score 3), to largely correct with minor flaws (score 4), and to fully aligned high-quality empathetic responses (score 5). This design introduces fine-grained variations in empathy quality, covering both coarse and subtle failure modes. In addition, we construct speech-level supervision by introducing mismatches between user emotion and response delivery. Specifically, we synthesize response audio with incorrect emotional styles using Qwen3-TTS [16] (e.g., excited or disgust tone for negative user states), enabling the model to learn cross-modal alignment. By combining content-level quality variation and audio-level inconsistency, we obtain approximately 200K training samples that jointly capture semantic empathy quality and speech-level alignment. 

D.2 Training Details 

We train the reward model based on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B [42], which supports joint modeling of text and audio inputs. The model takes as input the user query emotion label, query audio, and candidate response, and predicts two evaluation signals: a content-level score in the range [0, 5], reflecting the quality of empathetic response, and an audio-level score in 0, 1, indicating whether the speech delivery is consistent with the intended emotional context. 20 The model is trained for 2 epochs on the constructed dataset of approximately 200K samples. During training, all components, including the audio encoder, adapter, and LLM backbone, are jointly optimized. This setup enables the reward model to capture both fine-grained differences in empathetic quality and alignment between semantic content and vocal expression.
