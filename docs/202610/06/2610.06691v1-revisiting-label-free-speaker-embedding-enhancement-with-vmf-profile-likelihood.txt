# Abstract 

Embedding enhancement improves speaker verification under acoustic mismatch without modifying a frozen backbone. Re-cent work has established a practical label-free setting for this task, but often adopts increasingly structured formulations. Here, the clean target is directly observed during training, mak-ing enhancement a matching problem on the unit hypersphere. We model the clean target with a von Mises–Fisher (vMF) like-lihood and profile out a sample-wise concentration parameter, yielding a simple closed-form objective with adaptive weight-ing. Across VoxCeleb1, VoxSRC23, CN-Celeb, VOiCES, and VC-Mix, the proposed method largely preserves the baseline and gives clearer gains on challenging mismatch sets. It also remains stable under a broad single-view recipe, where a recent diffusion baseline becomes less reliable in controlled compar-isons. These results suggest that effective label-free embedding enhancement in this setting does not require a highly structured formulation. 

Index Terms : speaker verification, embedding enhancement, robust speaker representations 

# 1. Introduction 

Modern speaker verification systems map variable-length utter-ances to fixed-dimensional embeddings using deep neural net-works [1, 2, 3]. These embeddings are typically trained with margin-based classification objectives [4, 5] or contrastive ob-jectives [6, 7]. Verification is then performed by computing the cosine similarity between embeddings. Despite this progress, acoustic mismatch remains a critical challenge. Background noise, reverberation, microphone variations, and codec effects can severely degrade performance when test conditions differ from training conditions [8, 9, 10]. A common strategy is to build robustness directly into the pipeline through large-scale data [11, 12], extensive aug-mentation [13, 14, 15, 16], or specialized domain adapta-tion [17, 18, 19, 20]. These approaches can be effective, but they are expensive and closely tied to the pipeline. In many applications, retraining or fine-tuning a high-capacity speaker representation extractor for each new environment is impracti-cal. Embedding enhancement avoids full-network retraining by applying a lightweight post-processing network to a frozen ex-tractor. This approach is practical because it leaves the original scoring pipeline unchanged. Early work explored this direction through selective enhancement [21] and disentangled represen-tation learning (DRL) [22], though these methods often required

More recently, a generative approach based on diffusion models [23] has estab-lished a fully label-free paradigm, demonstrating that effective enhancement can be achieved using only clean and degraded utterances. While these recent advancements broaden the design space, they also move the field toward increasingly structured formu-lations. However, the fundamental appeal of embedding en-hancement is precisely its lightweight nature. In this setup, the backbone is frozen, the target is directly observed during train-ing, and inference requires only one refined embedding before cosine scoring. This makes it natural to ask how much formu-lation complexity is actually needed in this setting. We show that a simple direct matching objective is already effective when aligned with the geometry of normalized embed-dings. Since modern speaker embeddings are L2-normalized, they lie on the unit hypersphere [24, 13]. We model the clean target with a von Mises–Fisher (vMF) density and profile out a sample-wise concentration parameter, yielding a simple closed-form objective based on maximum likelihood estimation. The resulting loss exhibits a simple but useful property where pairs with larger residuals receive smaller updates. This is helpful when learning from heterogeneous data, reflecting the unpredictable severity of acoustic corruption found in real-world environments. Across two frozen backbones and seven evaluation sets, the proposed method largely preserves the base-line and gives clearer improvements on challenging mismatch benchmarks such as CN-Celeb, VOiCES, and VC-Mix. Fig-ure 1 provides an overview of both the practical paired-target setting and the variant-wise weighting behavior induced by vMF-PL on the unit hypersphere. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: • We revisit label-free speaker embedding enhancement in the frozen backbone setting, and show that a simple direct match-ing formulation is already competitive. • We derive a vMF profile likelihood objective for normalized embeddings, yielding a simple closed-form loss with adap-tive reweighting across training pairs. • Evaluations on VoxCeleb1 [11], VoxSRC23 [25], CN-Celeb [26], VOiCES [9], and VC-Mix [10] show that the proposed method mainly preserves or improves the base-line, with clearer gains on challenging benchmarks. Under a broad single-view recipe, it also remains stable, while a re-cent diffusion baseline becomes unreliable in controlled com-parisons.

# 2. Related Works 

2.1. Robust Speaker Verification 

Robustness to acoustic mismatch is a central issue in speaker verification [8, 9, 27, 10]. A standard strategy is to expose the backbone to broad acoustic variation during training [13], using large corpora such as VoxCeleb [11, 12] together with augmentations such as MUSAN [14], RIRs [15], and SpecAug-ment [16]. These approaches directly improve the extractor and maintain strong practical baselines. Other methods modify the network structure or training objective to handle nuisance variation more explicitly. Ex-amples include adversarial domain adaptation to align feature distributions across mismatched conditions [17, 18] and joint architectures that model speech enhancement or noise extrac-tion together with speaker verification [19]. Other recent work employs diffusion-based auto-encoding to disentangle speaker identity from frame-level speech content [20]. While effective, these methods remain tied to the full extraction pipeline. Adapt-ing them to new conditions usually requires retraining or fine-tuning the backbone. 

2.2. Embedding Enhancement 

To address this limitation, embedding enhancement applies a lightweight post-processing model to a frozen extractor. Early work explored this direction through selective enhance-ment [21], which uses a gating module to suppress nuisance factors while preserving clean-condition behavior. Subsequent work introduced disentangled representation learning (DRL) to the embedding space [22]. This approach relies on an encoder-decoder structure and adversarial training to separate speaker identity from nuisance factors. However, it requires explicit supervision, such as speaker or session (video ID) labels, limiting its practical scalability. More recently, SEED [23] established a fully label-free set-ting by training the post-processor solely on paired clean and degraded utterances. While this avoids the need for speaker la-bels, it introduces a generative diffusion process directly in the embedding space. To achieve inference efficiency, it relies on single-step sampling at a fixed timestep. It also introduces the strong assumption that the diffusion trajectories of noisy and clean embeddings are nearly identical. Our work keeps the same practical setting of a frozen back-bone, a low-capacity post-processor, and training from paired clean and degraded utterances, but replaces structured genera-tive modeling with a direct matching objective on normalized embeddings.

# 4. Experiments 

4.1. Evaluation Setup Datasets and metrics. We evaluate the proposed method using pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN [2] and ResNet-34 [12] as frozen backbones. The VoxCeleb1 test sets (Vox1-O, Vox1-E, and Vox1-H) [11] serve as the standard baselines for verification performance. To assess robustness under acoustic mismatch, we also evaluate on various challenging and out of domain bench-marks, including VoxSRC23 [25], CN-Celeb [26], VOiCES [9], and VC-Mix [10]. We report Equal Error Rate (EER) and min-imum Detection Cost Function (minDCF) with Ptarget = 0 .05 .

Comparative methods and architecture. We compare the frozen baseline (i.e., cosine scoring directly on the unenhanced embeddings), the recent diffusion-based SEED [23], and the proposed vMF-PL method. All post-processing systems use the same restricted capacity architecture, 3 blocks of residual MLP with hidden width 2p, LayerNorm, SiLU activations, and out-put L2 normalization. This keeps model capacity fixed and fo-cuses the comparison on the training formulation. The speaker embedding dimensions p of the ECAPA-TDNN and ResNet-34 are 512 and 256, respectively. For the main comparison in Table 1, the proposed method is trained with the broad single-view recipe described in Section 4.2. In contrast, SEED is re-produced using its official implementation under its published setup, which relies on a milder multi-view training pipeline and yields its strongest performance. We adopt this asymmetric practical comparison because, in our controlled experiments, SEED did not train reliably under the broad single-view recipe used for our method. We quantify this instability explicitly in the controlled same recipe comparison of Section 4.4 and Ta-ble 2. All models are trained on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU using the AdamW optimizer [30]. We set the learning rate to 5e-4 and apply a weight decay of 5e-5. 

4.2. Broad Single-View Training Recipe 

To expose the network to diverse acoustic variations, we con-struct a heterogeneous training pool using the VoxCeleb2 de-velopment set (2360 hours, 1092K utterances) [12], LibriTTS-R (244 hours, 149.7K utterances) [31], and Libri-Light (577 hours, 2.6K utterances) [32]. Since VoxCeleb2 dominates the utterance count, we randomly sample 20% of it per epoch to maintain domain balance. All training audio is sampled at 16 kHz and cropped to a maximum duration of 2.015 seconds. For each segment, we generate a clean-degraded pair using an overlapping augmenta-tion pipeline: • Environmental mismatch (50% prob. each): Additive noise and music from MUSAN [14] in the range of SNR 

[−20 , 20] dB, and simulated RIRs [15]. These are applied independently and can overlap. • Telephony degradation (20% prob.): 16 kHz to 8 kHz down-sampling, μ-law companding, and [−5, 5] dB random volume scaling. Our pipeline follows the comparative method, SEED [23], but differs in data composition and batching. SEED utilizes only LibriTTS-R and Libri-Light, applies augmentations with milder SNR, and adopts multi-view batch construction [6]. Be-cause our formulation lacks strict pipeline requirements, we adopt a memory-efficient single-view approach. Furthermore, by applying overlapping augmentations to this single view, we effectively simulate real-world degradations, e.g., background noise with reverberation. This strategy allows us to incorporate broader acoustic variations without the computational overhead of multi-view scaling.

4.4. Stability under a Unified Training Recipe 

To isolate the effect of the enhancement formulation from dataset composition and batch construction, Table 2 evaluates both methods under the same heterogeneous single-view recipe detailed in Section 4.2. Under this controlled setting, vMF-PL remains stable and reproduces the main result, whereas SEED does not train reliably. For example, its EER increases from 0.94% to 2.49% on Vox1-O, from 5.96% to 10.65% on VoxSRC23, and from 6.18% to 10.85% on VOiCES. This con-stitutes a severe degradation relative to both its published setup and the frozen baseline. We do not interpret this as a general limitation of diffu-sion models. Rather, it indicates that, in the lightweight post-processing regime considered here, performance is much more sensitive to recipe design for SEED than for vMF-PL. The pro-posed objective is substantially more robust under broader and less curated acoustic variation.

The difference between MSE and vMF-PL therefore lies not in the underlying geometry, but in how residuals ri =ˆxc,i − xc,i are aggregated across training pairs. MSE can be viewed as vMF maximum likelihood with a fixed concen-tration, whereas vMF-PL profiles a sample-wise concentration and thereby induces adaptive weighting. Concretely, vMF-PL scales the MSE update by 1/(∥ri∥22 + ϵ), so high-residual pairs contribute less than they would under MSE. This is es-pecially helpful when corruption severity varies widely across training pairs, as in real-world settings. The effect is reflected in Table 3. Replacing vMF-PL with MSE degrades Vox1-O EER from 0.88% to 1.05% and VoxSRC23 EER from 5.65% to 6.38%. The benefit therefore comes from profiled sample-wise weighting, not from additional model capacity or data. 

# 5. Conclusion 

We revisited label-free speaker embedding enhancement in the practical frozen-backbone setting. Because clean targets are di-rectly observed during training, we formulate enhancement as direct matching on normalized embeddings and derive a sim-ple vMF profile likelihood objective with adaptive weighting. Across two frozen backbones and seven benchmarks, the pro-posed method preserves the baseline performance, improves challenging mismatch sets, and remains more stable than both MSE and the recent diffusion baseline under the broad single-view recipe.
