ABSTRACT
Speech delivery varies with both the requested paralin-
guistic attribute and the linguistic content.
We introduce
ParaGeo, a matched-content decomposition of paralinguistic
variation in a frozen speech language model. Synthesized au-
dio tokens are replayed with a fixed listening prompt; pooled
key/value (K/V) representations are centered and projected
into a shared low-dimensional space.
Our GLM-4-Voice
probe spans 80 requested controls from 12 benchmark fami-
lies across eight sentences. With a globally fitted calibration
basis, content-held-out centroid accuracy is 9.49% versus a
1.25% permutation baseline; same-label cross-content co-
sine similarity is 0.285 versus 0.017, and both conditional
permutation tests yield p = 0.001. A separate ten-scenario,
six-style probe reveals reproducible contrast directions across
scenarios. Static, additive, and temporal interventions pro-
duce attribute-, layer-, and schedule-dependent response
profiles. These results provide a shared coordinate represen-
tation for measuring paralinguistic structure and an empirical
starting point for latent speech control. Code is available at
https://github.com/yuhanlydia/ParaGeo.
Index Terms— paralinguistic representation, latent ge-
ometry, speech language models, speech control
1. INTRODUCTION
A sentence can be spoken with high pitch, slow pace, warm
attitude, or hesitant delivery. Expressive speech therefore re-
quires controlling delivery while preserving the lexical mes-
sage. Learned style tokens, reference-conditioned prosody
transfer, and information bottlenecks provide reusable speech
factors [1, 2, 3]. Semantic–acoustic token hierarchies [4] and
speech-native dialogue models [5, 6] bring this question to
speech language models. We ask: which changes in vocal
delivery recur across contexts, and how can heterogeneous
attributes be described in a shared coordinate system?
In a ten-scenario debt-negotiation probe, six requested
styles yield reproducible key/value (K/V) differences (Fig. 1).

3. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Model and calibration. We use GLM-4-Voice-9B [5] with
int4/NF4 weights, temperature 0.8, top-p 0.8, and at most 768
generated tokens. Our probe crosses 80 benchmark-derived
requests with eight fixed sentences, yielding 640 requested
pairs and 590 retained representations after text matching
and audio-token availability checks. This is a requested-label
probe rather than an official recognition benchmark.
Fea-
tures are extracted from layers {16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36} with
rank 16. The content reference retains eight SVD columns,
although eight centered means span at most seven nonzero-
variance directions.
Representation tests. A cosine nearest-centroid classifier
predicts one of 80 exact labels, using seven sentences to form
centroids and the remaining sentence for testing. The basis
and unlabeled within-sentence centering use all calibration
features, making this a fixed-basis, content-held-out centroid
probe [23]. We also measure same-label cross-content cosine
similarity and raw-SVD explained variance. For 1,000 condi-
tional permutations, labels are independently shuffled within
each sentence; p = (1 + b)/1001, where b is the number of
null statistics at least as large as the observed value [24]. The
separate six-style discovery uses all 126 overlapping balanced
5/5 scenario splits, bidirectional evaluation, and Benjamini–
Hochberg FDR across 15 style contrasts [25]; these splits are
not independent replications.
Generation and controls.
Catalog-covered English
SpeechParaling inputs use the waveform path and model-
based pairwise evaluation protocol of SpeechParaling. Devel-
opment uses positions 0, 5, 10, . . . in the eligible-item list; all
other positions are held out. We select α ∈{0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5}
by maximizing development preference subject to a prespec-
ified two-percentage-point text-WER tolerance (∆WER ≤
0.02), breaking ties toward smaller scales.
Static steering
selects α = 0.5. Composition uses α = 0.25 exploratorily
because no scale satisfies the tolerance; dynamic steering
uses α = 0.25 exploratorily because the selected scale 1.5 is
incomplete. Evaluation sets contain 168 static, 38 composi-
tion, and 60 dynamic items; ablations use the first 24 held-out
items in benchmark-index order.
The reference is matched prompt-only generation. Full-
space composition sums the same prototypes and matches the
global norm of the projected vector. A Gaussian unit direc-
tion provides norm-matched controls; its orientation is fixed
per dynamic item, while its norm follows the target schedule.
Judgments are scored as 1 for a win, 0.5 for a tie, and 0 for a
loss; scores are first averaged within an item and then across
items. A preference score of 50 denotes parity. Nominal 95%
intervals are computed using 10,000 item-level bootstrap re-
samples [26]. A balanced extension reuses 180 ParaGeo out-
puts (18 labels, ten inputs each) against five direction-control
seeds; these are not 900 independent inputs.

5. CONCLUSION
We identified recurring paralinguistic structure in GLM-4-
Voice and introduced ParaGeo to represent it in shared coor-
dinates. Cross-scenario contrasts, fixed-basis centroid probes,
and conditional permutation tests support this structure. The
projection increases same-label alignment while reducing
fine-grained discrimination; generation tests reveal attribute-,
layer-, and schedule-dependent responses.
A shared basis
makes vocal delivery an explicit object for comparison and
editing, as attributes, combinations, and temporal paths reuse
the same coordinates without updating the model. This pro-
vides an empirical starting point for latent speech control by
separating the representation of delivery from its generation-
time application. Future work will target training-only basis
fitting, cross-model replication, and listener validation.

A. EXTENDED RESULTS
A.1. A shared representation with measured intervention
responses
ParaGeo makes a heterogeneous set of vocal requests compa-
rable within one frozen speech model. The central object is
a common projection of replay-conditioned K/V representa-
tions. An attribute, a combination of attributes, and a tempo-
ral transition can all be expressed with that projection. The
extended results answer two connected questions: whether
requested delivery has recurring structure across lexical con-
texts, and how updates constructed from that structure affect
generation. Keeping those questions explicit makes the favor-
able results interpretable without requiring every intervention
to improve the aggregate benchmark score.
The strongest representation evidence is the conjunction
of cross-content decoding, same-label alignment, and a con-
ditional permutation control. In the 590-record calibration
collection, projected exact-label accuracy is 9.49%, com-
pared with a permutation mean of 1.25%; same-label cosine
is 0.285, compared with 0.017. Both finite permutation esti-
mates are 1/1001 ≈0.001. The raw rank-16 SVD captures
61.76% of residual variance. These measurements support a
compact, shared description of requested delivery under the
acquisition protocol. The final projection also changes which
distinctions are retained:
same-label alignment increases
while exact-label decoding decreases relative to centered full
K/V.
The generation results locate useful responses within that
representation. Middle-layer dynamic injection has a prefer-
ence estimate of 68.75 on the fixed diagnostic subset, with a
nominal interval of [56.25,81.25] and a text-WER change of
−0.0060. In the balanced static study, tired and polite have
means of 71 and 64 against five direction controls, and both
remain above parity in every control column. Pace transi-
tions score 75 on the 12 corresponding dynamic inputs. Each
finding identifies an observed setting in which the coordinates
produce a favorable response.
Their full comparison sets,
sampling units, and uncertainty appear below.
The aggregate results set the scope of those responses.
The direct static comparison against five random-direction
controls is 51.50 [45.39,57.56].
Composition reduces text
disturbance relative to norm-matched full-space addition, but
does not win the direct preference comparison. The com-
plete dynamic evaluation uses an exploratory scale because
the development-selected arm is incomplete. The resulting
scientific account is a shared-coordinate representation with
attribute- and operator-dependent response profiles; the evi-
dence does not establish an overall controller advantage over
the qualified direction controls.
A.2. Discovery in spoken interaction
The initial discovery collection contains ten debt-negotiation
scenarios, two generation seeds, and six vocal styles: 120
unique representations.
Across all 126 overlapping bal-
anced 5/5 scenario partitions, evaluated in both directions,
audio-token mean pooling gives a median six-way accuracy
of 85.0%, compared with 52.92% for the last audio token
and 16.7% uniform chance. Every partition exceeds its re-
ported shuffled 95th-percentile reference.
All 15 oriented
style contrasts pass the reported Benjamini–Hochberg proce-
dure at FDR 0.05 [25]. Their mean cross-scenario direction
consistency is 0.498; firm–hesitant reaches 0.803, whereas
calm–neutral is 0.137.
These measurements motivate pooled acquisition:
the
same requested change can recur across scenarios even when
lexical content changes. Contrast consistency is the cosine
between the same oriented style difference in different sce-
narios, rather than similarity between the two named styles.
The repeated partitions reuse utterances and are not indepen-
dent replications.
Six-way scenario decoding and 80-way
sentence decoding use different label spaces and preprocess-
ing. Historical negotiation search did not establish a reliable
terminal-utility gain; it supplies the discovery context, rather
than a downstream success claim for ParaGeo.
The com-
plete contrast matrix, repeated-split confusion counts, and
historical task summaries remain in the supplied data.

D.3. Temporal schedules
For a requested transition a →b, ct = (1 −λt)ca + λtcb.
Gradual interpolation uses λt = min(t/191, 1); the sudden
schedule switches from zero to one at forward pass 96. Start-
only, end-only, and midpoint-only use constant coefficients
zero, one, and one-half. The endpoint is held after the 192-
pass schedule. Figure A1 shows the prescribed coefficients.
Early termination can prevent a trajectory from reaching
its endpoint, and a forward-pass midpoint need not be the
acoustic midpoint. A preference response to the requested
transition therefore does not verify its waveform timing. The
constant-coordinate controls test whether the implemented
path contributes beyond one fixed point under the same gen-
eration interface.
D.4. Representation and intervention have distinct de-
pendencies
Replay features summarize listening to an utterance; the up-
date acts while producing new text/audio tokens. Their model
weights are shared, but prompts, token roles, and causal states
differ.
Locally, an output statistic f(q) responds approxi-
mately as f(q + δ) −f(q) ≈Jf(q)δ. The covariance used
to fit B⊥does not identify this Jacobian. A decodable direc-
tion can consequently have a weak or unintended generation
effect [21, 22]. This is a distinction between estimands, not a
measured diagnosis of the aggregate controller result.
For an ordinary attention query, adding a common post-
position-transform key offset to every unmasked source po-
sition shifts all logits equally and leaves their softmax un-
changed. A common value offset instead adds that offset to
the weighted value sum.
These identities require uniform
offsets at the stated locations. A pre-attention fused-QKV
hook, positional transformations, selective token updates,
and caching need not satisfy those assumptions. They clarify
why K/V intervention details matter without implying that
the recorded hook is inert.

Evaluation uses catalog-covered English SpeechParaling-

H.1. What low-rank composition changes
The 38-input composition comparison holds the summed pro-
totype construction and concatenated intervention norm fixed
while comparing projected and full-space directions (Ap-
pendix D). Text WER is 0.45671 with projection and 0.50280
in full space, a descriptive reduction of 4.61 percentage
points. Prompt-only WER is 0.38789, so both interventions
still disturb the text channel relative to that reference. No
paired WER interval is supplied for the projected–full-space
difference.
Direct preference for the projected direction is 42.98
[34.87,51.10].
Of the 38 item-averaged scores, eight are
above parity, 17 equal, and 13 below. Their 88 dimension
decisions comprise ten wins, 56 ties, and 22 losses.
The
denominators refer to different units and are both retained.
No reported family in the direct comparison exceeds 50. Pro-
jection therefore yields less text disturbance at this matched
operating point, accompanied by a lower control-preference
estimate; it is not demonstrated compositional superiority.
The 24-input configuration study in Appendix F is a dif-
ferent subset. Its all-layer preference of 66.67 cannot replace
the complete 38-input estimate of 45.39. Together, the two re-
sults show that favorable local preference and complete-task
performance can differ, and that composition scale remains
constrained by text fidelity. The mean and normalized-sum
ablations help characterize that dependence, but their changed
magnitude prevents a pure test of semantic compositionality.

I.2. What the supplied package can reproduce
The package provides aggregate geometry and confidence
bounds, complete configuration scores, per-family sum-
maries, the 18-by-5 response matrix, and the historical
discovery matrices.
They support the native tables and
summary-level plots.
The raw K/V NPZ files, every gen-
erated waveform, the full singular spectrum, per-item judge
outputs, and all permutation draws are not present in the at-
tached packet. The recorded intervals are retained without
fabricating replacements from means. A true 80-by-80 proto-
type geometry, uncapped energy rank, or measured acoustic
trajectory requires the original local artifacts. The local ex-
porter can process those artifacts when available; its presence
does not imply it has been run on them.
I.3. Positioning the contribution against existing controls
Learned style tokens and reference-conditioned prosody
transfer make expressive speech factors reusable [1, 2];
SpeechSplit and SpeechTokenizer separate speech infor-
mation through learned bottlenecks or token structure [3, 4].
ParaGeo studies a post-hoc shared projection in a frozen
model’s speech-replay K/V representation.
This describes
the object investigated without asserting that learned style
factors or latent arithmetic are new.
Activation addition and contrastive addition already con-
struct inference-time behavior directions [8, 9], and the lin-
ear representation hypothesis connects probing and steer-
ing through geometry [10].
In speech, EmoSteer-TTS se-
lects emotion-relevant token contributions; EmoShift learns
emotion-specific activation offsets; and CoCoEmo constructs
matched emotion contrasts and analyzes steering locations

I.4. Boundaries of the conclusions
The experiments cover one backbone, English generation
requests, a fixed calibration lexical bank, and model-based
judgments.
The balanced extension reuses one synthetic
input voice. Requested labels are not independently anno-
tated realized attributes, and emitted-text matching/WER is
not waveform transcription.
The globally fitted basis, im-
perfect content proxy, unadjusted exploratory configuration
intervals, reused control comparisons, and missing evaluator
version limit the respective generalization and replication
claims. These restrictions attach to specific measurements,
rather than cancel the observed request-associated structure
or favorable response settings.
Age, Cognitive State, and Attitude are generation-request
labels. A coordinate for confident tone does not measure a
real person’s confidence, and an elderly-voice instruction is
not a validated age estimator. The record supplies no clini-
cal, demographic, or personality inference. Applications to
speaker profiling or consequential decisions would require
different evidence and ethical assessment.
The extended results make the practical implication pre-
cise: a common coordinate system allows vocal delivery to be
compared, combined, and scheduled with fixed speech-model
weights; choosing an effective intervention still requires at-
tention to the attribute, injection location, norm, and temporal
interface. Training-only basis evaluation, independent acous-
tic/listener validation, exact evaluator recovery, and fresh con-
firmation of the favorable settings remain separate evidence
obligations.
